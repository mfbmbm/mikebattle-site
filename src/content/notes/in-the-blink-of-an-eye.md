- 'Editing… is not so much a putting together as it is a discovery of a path.'
- 'What they finally remember is not the editing, not the camerawork, not the performances, not even the story - it's how they felt.'
- 'Always try to do the most with the least.'
- 'Suggestion is always more effective than exposition.'
- 'Frequently, it takes more work and discernment to decide where not to cut.'
- Each editor makes a different film from the same footage.
- The overactive editor is a tour guide who can't stop pointing. Sometimes you just want to walk around and see what you see.
- Cut the movie it wants to be. A chimp and a human start out alike, then evolve apart. Don't try to cut a chimp movie into a human one.
- The ideal cut, in order of priority: emotion, story, rhythm, eye-trace, planarity, continuity of space. When something has to give, sacrifice from the bottom up. Get emotion and story right and the audience won't notice the rest.
- The editor's job is to sit in the audience's seat. What are they thinking? Where are they looking? What should they feel?
- The editor is freed from the context of creation: no idea what a shot cost or what happened at the edge of the frame. Just like the audience.
- If most of an audience hates one scene, the scene is probably fine. They likely missed something they needed five minutes earlier.
- Cuts work because they resemble dreams. We say 'it's just a dream' the way parents say 'it's just a film'.
- A perfect film feels like thought, projected from your own eyes. Each blink is a cut.
- People blink when they get what you're saying, when one thought ends and the next begins. Where you'd blink is where the cut goes.
- Cutting *The Conversation*, Murch kept finding Gene Hackman blinked exactly where he wanted to cut. Hackman was thinking the character's thoughts.
- Someone who blinks in the wrong places makes a conversation feel off. Same with cuts.
- Cuts mimic 'the acrobatic nature of thought itself'. Close-ups show a character's thoughts at the closest level.
- Every film has its own rhythmic signature.
- Coppola and Lucas preferred modern electronic editing systems as it offered more creative options.
- Digital editing made things more flexible but was also problematic as it allowed later decision making.
- The proliferation of editing technology does mean 'anyone' can make a great film. 'All of us today are able to walk into an art store and buy inexpensive pigments and supplies that the Renaissance painters would have paid fortunes for. And yet, do any of us paint on their level today?'
- Filmmaking is almost impossible for one person to control. Coppola: 'The director is the ringmaster of a circus that is inventing itself.'
- 'At home, you are king, and the television is your jester. If you are not amused, you take out the remote control and chop off his head! The framework of home viewing is familiarity: What is right is what fits with the routine, which implies a mind-set that sees only what it wants - or is prepared - to see.'
