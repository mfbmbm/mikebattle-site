- 'That was my first lesson in the business - who you know matters.'
- 'Nothing in Hollywood is anything until it's something. And the way to show it's something is belief.'
- 'Hollywood has always been a ruthless business.'
- 'It's only blarney if you can't make it happen.'
- 'We weren't in a creative medium, we were in a commercially creative medium.'
- Perception is everything. CAA agents 'reached for the club' every day but never used it.
- Pick a persona you can inhabit without strain.
- Image as strategy: Armani suits, red script covers all over LA, hired Jaguars when they had no money.
- Every film lives and dies on belief. Lose one champion and it can collapse.
- He bluffed a cease and desist away in two hours. Blarney, but he made it happen.
- Learn the terrain. He watched every Oscar winner in the top five categories, and studied Japanese culture before the Sony deal.
- 'If Paul Newman liked clams I liked clams.'
- Honesty cuts through. He told Sean Connery he was taking bad project after bad project. The next film was a hit.
- Negotiating: instil the negative in advance, plan the end point, expect buyer's and seller's remorse.
- CAA's four rules: never lie to clients or colleagues, return every call the same day, follow up, don't bad-mouth the competition.
- Everyone at CAA was a partner, mail room up. In a big company you start to believe you are the company.
- 'Directors had stars in their pockets.' Ask younger people what's next.
- In meetings, 'I'll look into that' beats answering on the spot. Make everyone feel comfortable and important.
- Pitch with motion and emotion. Understate. 'Mystique is 10 times better than publicity.'
- CAA spent $500,000 a year on gifts; Ovitz wrote around 1,000 letters a year. But the chits add up.
- Hollywood is zero-sum: everyone fighting over one pile of money.
- He got out of film as TV took over.
- He signed Dustin Hoffman by pitching his small client list as more time for him.
- He fought to get *Shogun* made when no one would greenlight it.
- Also read: *When I Stop Talking, You'll Know I'm Dead*, Jerry Weintraub.
