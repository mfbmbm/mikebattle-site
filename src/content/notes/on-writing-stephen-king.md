## Quotes

- "Write anything you want as long as you tell the truth."
- "Do you need someone to make you a paper badge with the word writer on it before you can believe you are one? God, I hope not."
- "Never tell us a thing if you can show us instead."
- "To write is human, to edit is divine."
- "When you write a story, you're telling yourself the story. When you rewrite, your main job is taking out all the things that are not the story."
- "The hours we spend talking about writing is time we don't spend actually doing it."
- "I'm convinced that fear is at the root of most bad writing."
- "Don't wait for the muse."

## Lessons

- **Start by copying.** Imitation precedes creation. King's first stories were copycat hybrids.
- **Recognise ideas when they show up.** They arrive from nowhere, like fossils in the ground. Dig them out with delicate tools, not a jackhammer.
- **The situation comes first.** Put characters in an interesting predicament. Don't worry about the ending yet.
- **Write what you know and tell the truth.** It feeds into everything else.
- **Rejection is inevitable.** King stacked his rejection slips on a nail, then replaced it with a spike and kept writing.
- **Don't be ashamed of your genre.** If you write, someone will try to make you feel lousy about it.
- **Write with the door closed, rewrite with the door open.** Leave the first draft for six weeks before you go back to it.
- **Second draft = first draft minus 10%.** Rewriting is taking out everything that isn't the story.
- **Fight the outside pressure.** His mother told him to get a teaching qualification to fall back on, and he spent years in dead-end jobs before the break.
- **Let your loved ones in.** Writing is lonely. His wife Tabby never voiced a single doubt, and pulled the first pages of *Carrie* out of the bin.
- **Make a daily routine.** Four to six hours of reading and writing, ideally 1,000 words a day, six days a week. Make sure the muse knows where you'll be. Read, because you'll read terrible writers and realise you can do it.
- **Every character is you.** Let them speak the truth, swear included. Show, don't tell. Listen to how people really talk.
- **Story first, theme second.** Look for the underlying patterns once the draft is done, then rewrite to bring them out. Symbolism should adorn, not fake profundity.
- **Be your own advocate.** Agents and publishers are all looking for the next hot writer.

## The writer's toolbox

- **Vocabulary:** use the first word that comes to mind. Dressing up short words in long ones is like dressing a pet in evening clothes.
- **Grammar:** know the rules before you break them. Fragments are fine. Grammar is the pole you grab to get your thoughts up on their feet.
- **Verbs:** choose active over passive ("The meeting's at seven"). The adverb is not your friend.
- **Paragraphs:** the paragraph, not the sentence, is the basic unit of writing. Easy reads have short paragraphs, and the same goes for scripts. Words have weight.

**Also read:** *The Elements of Style* by William Strunk Jr. and E. B. White.
